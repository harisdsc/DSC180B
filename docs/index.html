Introduction

Why Traditional Credit Scoring
Falls Short

Credit scores play a critical role in shaping financial opportunities, influencing access to loans, credit cards, and housing. However, traditional systems are inherently biased toward individuals who already have established credit histories.

This creates a structural disadvantage for the credit-invisible (young adults, recent immigrants, and low-income backgrounds) who may have limited or no prior credit activity.

Most people, however, maintain active bank accounts containing valuable information about their real-time income, spending habits, and financial stability.

Cash-flow underwriting leverages this everyday transaction data to evaluate borrowers more fairly. Our project applies NLP and machine learning to transaction records, creating a "Cash Score" that predicts credit risk without relying on traditional credit history.

Fairer Assessments

Evaluate creditworthiness based on real financial behavior rather than repayment history alone.

Data-Driven Insights

Analyze spending, income, and balance dynamics across multiple time windows to build predictive features.

Privacy First

All datasets are de-identified. Analysis focuses on behavioral patterns, not individual identity.

Methods

Data Wrangling & Feature Engineering

01

Data Cleaning

Technical Details
  • Held out ~3,000 consumers with null delinquency labels for out-of-sample evaluation
  • Removed credit_score variable to avoid biasing toward traditional scoring
  • Filtered transactions occurring after each consumer's evaluation date
  • Applied minimum data sufficiency criteria: at least 1 linked account, 3 months of history, and identifiable recurring income
02

Feature Engineering

Feature Categories
  • Account-Level: Total/average balances, account diversity, liquidity indicators, negative balance exposure, wealth tier flags
  • Balance Dynamics: Reconstructed running balances capturing volatility, daily changes, drawdowns, balance trends (30/90/365 days), overdraft flags
  • Income: Total/average monthly income, income volatility, number of sources, regularity, recency of income events
  • Spending Ratios: Category-level spend-to-income ratios across 1, 3, 6, and 9-month windows
03

Feature Selection

04

Model Training

Model Details

Logistic Regression: 80-20 train-test split, StandardScaler preprocessing, class balance weighting. Evaluated with AUC-ROC.

XGBoost: Gradient boosted decision trees with grid search cross-validation. Uses scale_pos_weight for class imbalance. Evaluated on AUC-ROC and per-sample latency.
